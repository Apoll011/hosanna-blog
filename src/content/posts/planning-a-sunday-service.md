# Planning a Sunday service in Hosanna (songs are not the whole plan)

A setlist of five songs is not a service. Someone still has to welcome people, read scripture, preach, and make announcements—often with notes the band never sees.

Hosanna's service model is closer to a run of show than a Spotify playlist.

## What a service contains

A service has a date, a name, and an ordered list of **elements**. Element types include songs plus moments like welcome, scripture, message, and announcements.

That matters for the people on stage:

- Musicians open the service and swipe through items in order.
- Non-song items still show notes so the leader is not juggling a second app.
- Song items deep-link into the ChordPro reader with the transposition you set for that service.

## A sane planning order

1. **Create the service** with the correct date (filters later depend on this).
2. **Block the skeleton** — welcome, songs, scripture, message, closing—before obsessing over keys.
3. **Attach songs** from the library. Prefer songs that already have clean ChordPro.
4. **Add leader notes** where someone will actually read them under pressure.
5. **Walk the order once** on a phone the way a musician will.

If an item has no songs yet, that is fine. Empty plans show up in filters so you can find unfinished Sundays.

## Capo and transpose belong to the service song

A guitarist may capo for one service and not the next. Hosanna keeps transpose/capo scoped to the song **inside that service**, so last week's +2 does not haunt next week by accident.

## Agenda vs services

Agenda events cover calendar items and responsibilities. Services cover the worship order. Use both when your church schedules volunteers and music in the same week—but do not stuff volunteer rosters into ChordPro comments.

## Keep the plan boring

The goal is not a clever UI. The goal is that at 09:55 nobody asks which app has the real order. One service, one sequence, charts that open offline.
