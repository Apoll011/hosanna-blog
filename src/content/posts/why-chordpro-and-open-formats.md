# Why Hosanna is ChordPro-native (and allergic to lock-in)

Churches outlive apps. The songs should too.

We chose ChordPro early—not because it is trendy, but because a song stored as text survives the tool that wrote it. You can email it. You can put it in git. You can open it in SongBook Pro, OnSong, or a plain editor when Hosanna is not around.

## What "native" means here

Hosanna does not convert your library into a mystery binary and offer ChordPro as an export afterthought. The working format **is** ChordPro. Rendering, transpose, diagrams, and PDF export all start from that text.

Studio and the mobile apps sync that repertoire for the team. They do not redefine ownership.

## Open source where it counts

Parts of the Hosanna stack are open source; the product direction is public enough that musicians can see what we are building. Licensing details live on the site and repos—not in a blog slogan.

The point for a worship pastor is simpler: **you are not renting your lyrics from us.** You are using infrastructure to share charts your church already has the right to use.

## Against the binder-as-a-service trap

Some tools only feel fast while you stay subscribed, because leaving means retyping hundreds of songs. That is not a feature. That is an exit tax.

ChordPro keeps the exit door unlocked on purpose. We still want you to stay—because sync, services, roles, and offline reading are genuinely useful—not because your data is stuck.

## Practical takeaway

When you evaluate worship software, ask: *If this company vanished tomorrow, could I rebuild Sunday from the files I have?*

If the answer depends on a proprietary database dump, keep looking. If the answer is a folder of `.cho` / ChordPro text, you are closer to a sane foundation—whether or not you pick Hosanna.
