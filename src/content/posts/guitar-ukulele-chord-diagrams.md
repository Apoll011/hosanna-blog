# Guitar and ukulele chord diagrams without a second app

Chord diagrams are optional. Plenty of players read the ChordPro symbols and never look at a fretboard drawing. For newer musicians—or anyone jumping between guitar and ukulele—the diagram next to the chord name removes a trip to another app.

## Instruments and tunings

In reading settings you pick an instrument. Fretted instruments currently include:

**Guitar**

- Standard (EADGBE)
- Drop D
- 7-string (low B muted when the shape is a normal 6-string voicing)

**Ukulele**

- High G
- Low G
- Baritone (DGBE), using the same treble-side shapes guitarists already know

Piano is a separate path: highlighted keys, not frets. Capo does not apply there.

## Capo and transpose

Diagrams follow the **sounding** situation the reader is set to. If you capo 2 and leave transpose alone, shapes move relative to the capo as nut—the same mental model as a physical capo clamp on the neck.

String name labels under the diagram are optional. Turn them on while you are learning; turn them off when the screen feels crowded.

## What the shapes are (and are not)

We ship curated open shapes plus movable barre forms. Not every jazz extension has a perfect grip. When we cannot voice a chord honestly, the UI fails closed rather than inventing nonsense frets.

Finger numbers inside the dots are a preference. Some teachers want them; some stage charts look cleaner without.

## Practical tip for mixed teams

Put the default instrument in setup (guitar vs ukulele vs piano). Individuals can still change per device. The song file stays ChordPro either way—diagrams are a rendering choice, not a second source of truth.
